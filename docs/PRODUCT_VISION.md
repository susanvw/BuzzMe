# BuzzMe — Product Vision

*The founding manifesto. Every product and engineering decision should trace back to something in this document. If a proposed feature doesn't, that's a signal to stop and ask why.*

---

## 1. Vision Statement

A world where nobody misses the moments that matter to the people they love — not because they set a reminder for themselves, but because someone who cared remembered *with* them.

BuzzMe exists to make remembering a shared act, not a solitary chore.

---

## 2. Mission Statement

To give every family, team, and circle of friends a single, effortless, shared place to remember life's important moments — created in seconds, understood instantly, and felt as care rather than obligation.

---

## 3. Core Philosophy

> "People don't forget because they don't care. They forget because memory isn't shared."

This is the sentence that justifies BuzzMe's existence. Everything else in this document is a derivative of it.

Forgetting a vet appointment, a friend's birthday, or that the bins go out on Tuesday is not a character flaw and not a productivity failure. It's an architecture failure — the information lived in one person's head, or one person's calendar, when it should have lived *between* people.

BuzzMe's founding bet is that the unit of remembering should be the **relationship**, not the individual. A birthday isn't "my" reminder or "your" reminder — it's the household's, the family's, the team's. When memory is shared, it becomes resilient: if one person forgets, the board still remembers.

This reframes the entire category. BuzzMe is not competing to be a better personal organizer. It is creating a new category: **shared memory infrastructure for the people in your life.**

---

## 4. Product Principles

These are the non-negotiable filters every feature, screen, and word of copy must pass through.

1. **Simplicity above everything.** If a feature makes the app 1% more powerful but 5% harder to understand, it loses.
2. **Zero learning curve.** A new user should never need an explanation, tutorial, or tooltip to understand what to do next.
3. **Mobile-first, always.** Reminders happen in the flow of real life — on the go, between tasks, in a rush. The phone is the primary and most-honored surface.
4. **Friendly and human, never corporate.** BuzzMe should sound like a thoughtful friend, not a workplace tool. No jargon, no "productivity" language, no urgency-manufacturing.
5. **Shared by default, private by choice.** The natural gravity of the product pulls toward togetherness; privacy is always available but never the default assumption of the product's soul.
6. **Minimal screens.** Every additional screen is a place a user can get lost, confused, or bored. Fewer screens, deeper trust in each one.
7. **One Board, one shared truth.** Every reminder belongs to exactly one Board; every Member of that Board sees it and is notified about it the same way as everyone else — no per-person settings, no picking who's included. If different people need different reminders, that's a new Board, not a new setting.
8. **Beautiful enough to enjoy opening every day.** BuzzMe should feel like a small daily pleasure, not a chore app you dread checking.
9. **Fast enough to capture a thought before it's lost.** Under 10 seconds from "I should remember this" to "it's remembered." Speed is a feature, not an optimization.
10. **Cross-platform from day one.** The people you care about aren't all on the same device. A shared memory tool that excludes Android, iPhone, or web users has already failed at its core purpose.

## 5. Primary User Personas

**The Family Connector**
Usually a parent or grandparent. Holds the mental load for the household — appointments, kids' activities, family birthdays, chores. Exhausted by being the only one who remembers everything. Wants the burden distributed, not just organized.

**The Caring Partner**
In a relationship or marriage. Wants to show up for the things that matter to their partner — anniversaries, family events, small but meaningful dates — without relying on their partner to remind them.

**The Team Organizer**
Runs a rugby team, book club, or community group. Needs the whole group aligned on recurring and one-off events without becoming the group's unpaid secretary, chased for details in five different group chats.

**The Long-Distance Carer**
Adult child managing an aging parent's appointments remotely, or a co-parent coordinating across households. Needs shared visibility into things they can't be physically present for.

**The Quietly Overwhelmed Individual**
Juggles many small, easy-to-forget personal obligations (medication, filters, renewals) alongside shared ones. Wants one calm place for both, not ten different apps.

Across all personas, the common thread is not "I need to be more organized." It's **"I don't want to be the only one who remembers"** — or, for the individual, "I don't want to carry every small thing alone."

---

## 6. Problems BuzzMe Solves

- The mental load of being the sole "rememberer" in a family, relationship, or group.
- Important moments quietly missed because they lived in only one person's head or one person's calendar app.
- The awkwardness and friction of chasing people in group chats for dates, times, and confirmations.
- The gap between "personal to-do apps" (which don't share well) and "team calendars" (which feel like work).
- The cognitive overhead of translating a thought like *"vet's tomorrow at 4"* into structured calendar fields.
- The fragmentation of reminders across sticky notes, group chats, calendar apps, and memory.
- The emotional flatness of existing reminder tools — they alert, but they don't feel like care.
- The friction of inviting non-technical people (parents, grandparents, teammates) into a shared system.

---

## 7. Problems BuzzMe Intentionally Does NOT Solve

Being disciplined about what BuzzMe refuses to be is as important as what it commits to.

- **It is not a scheduling or meeting-booking tool.** No availability finding, no calendar-slot negotiation, no "find a time that works for everyone."
- **It is not a task or project management system.** No subtasks, no assignees-with-status, no kanban, no progress tracking, no deadlines-with-dependencies.
- **It is not a full calendar replacement.** It doesn't aim to hold every meeting, block time, or manage a professional schedule.
- **It is not a social network.** No feeds, no likes, no public posting culture, no follower counts, no algorithmic timeline.
- **It is not a messaging app.** Boards are for remembering things together, not for open-ended conversation. Any commentary features must stay lightweight and in service of the reminder, never become a chat replacement.
- **It is not a productivity or self-optimization tool.** No streaks, no productivity scores, no gamified "get more done" pressure.
- **It does not aim to be comprehensive.** It resists feature creep that would make it "everything app for organizing your life." It stays devoted to one job: shared remembering.

Every one of these refusals protects the core promise. The moment BuzzMe tries to also be a calendar, a task manager, or a group chat, it becomes forgettable itself — one more crowded app competing for attention instead of a calm, trusted companion.

---

## 8. Why BuzzMe Is Different

**vs. Google Calendar / Apple Calendar**
Calendars are built around *time* — slots, durations, scheduling conflicts, meeting invites. BuzzMe is built around *memory* — the fact that something matters, and who else it matters to. A calendar asks "when." BuzzMe asks "who needs to remember this together." Calendars are also inherently personal-first with sharing bolted on; BuzzMe is shared-first with privacy layered on top.

**vs. Todoist / Microsoft To Do**
These are personal productivity tools optimized for individual task completion — checkboxes, priorities, projects. Their sharing models are secondary and feel like collaboration features grafted onto a personal system. BuzzMe was never personal-first; the shared board is the native unit, not an afterthought.

**vs. WhatsApp / group chats**
Group chats are extraordinary at communication and terrible at memory. Important dates get buried in scrolling conversation within minutes and are gone forever. BuzzMe takes the *coordination instinct* people already use group chats for and gives it permanence, structure, and quiet, well-timed resurfacing — without the noise of an open conversation thread.

**The single differentiator underneath all of these:** every other tool treats sharing as a feature added to an individual product. BuzzMe treats *individual* use as a feature added to a fundamentally shared product. That inversion is the whole company.

## 9. The Emotional Experience We Want Users to Have

- **Relief**, the first time someone else remembers something so they didn't have to.
- **Warmth**, seeing a gentle notification that feels like a friend nudging them, not a system alerting them.
- **Trust**, that what they share on a private board stays within that board.
- **Delight**, at how little effort it took to capture something that used to require opening an app, tapping through fields, and choosing a repeat rule.
- **Belonging**, from being part of boards with the people who matter to them.
- **Calm**, never urgency. BuzzMe should lower anxiety about forgetting, not add another source of notification stress.
- **Being cared for**, which is the deepest emotional outcome: BuzzMe should make people feel like *someone thought of them*, even when that someone is a shared system doing the remembering on a group's behalf.

If a user ever describes BuzzMe as "stressful," "cluttered," "corporate," or "just another app I have to manage," the product has failed regardless of what features shipped successfully.

---

## 10. Design Philosophy

- **Conversational input over structured forms.** The default way to create a reminder is to say or type it naturally. Forms are a fallback, not the front door.
- **Warmth over efficiency-signaling.** BuzzMe is allowed to feel slower and softer than a productivity tool if it feels kinder. It is not competing on throughput.
- **Restraint over completeness.** Every screen should feel like it could not lose a single element without becoming less clear. If it can, that element shouldn't be there.
- **Familiarity over novelty.** People should recognize how to use BuzzMe instantly because it borrows the comfortable patterns of messaging and social apps they already know — not because it teaches them a new mental model.
- **One clear action per moment.** Users should never face an ambiguous "what do I do here" pause.
- **Personality without frivolity.** The bee/"buzz" motif and friendly tone should feel like character, not gimmick — present in language and small moments, never getting in the way of clarity.
- **Show, don't configure.** Where a productivity tool would offer a settings toggle, BuzzMe should try to infer the right behavior first, and offer the toggle only when inference genuinely isn't good enough.

## 11. Long-Term Product Vision

In its earliest form, BuzzMe is the calm, shared place where families and small groups remember life's important moments together — a Board, some Members, and the reminders they've told each other about, created in seconds with a short, honest form.

Over time, that foundation extends outward without ever abandoning the core philosophy — and, deliberately, without ever making BuzzMe depend on AI to be complete:

- From typing every reminder by hand, to **importing** ones that already exist somewhere else — a birthday already in your Contacts, a public holiday, a school term — always one-way, always something the person explicitly asked BuzzMe to go look for, never a background sync running on its own.
- From individual moments, to a living shared history — a family's collective memory of birthdays, milestones, and traditions, easy to look back on with warmth.
- From one language and one region, to a natural, human product usable anywhere, by anyone, regardless of technical comfort — including people who have never used a productivity app in their life.
- If natural-language reminder creation is ever added, it is a second way to fill in the same short form — never a different mental model, and never something the product's success depends on.

The throughline across every future direction: BuzzMe grows by getting better at *removing typing*, never by growing more complex to operate, and never by asking a person to trust a guess. Success is not "more features." Success is a grandparent, a teenager, and a rugby coach all using the same app comfortably on day one, with no one needing it explained to them.

## 12. Non-Functional Qualities

**Privacy**
Privacy is foundational, not a settings-page afterthought. Boards are private by default to their members. Personal reminders are never visible to anyone else, ever. Users control discoverability, who can invite them, who can mention them, and who can message them. Nothing about a user's data is used in ways they haven't clearly and simply consented to.

**Simplicity**
Every screen, flow, and piece of copy is held to the standard of "would a first-time user with no explanation know exactly what to do?" Simplicity is treated as a feature to be actively defended against feature creep, not a starting state that erodes over time.

**Performance**
The app must feel instant. Creating a reminder, opening a board, and receiving a notification should never feel like they are waiting on a system — the feeling of speed is part of the feeling of care.

**Accessibility**
BuzzMe is used across generations — grandparents, teenagers, people with varying technical comfort and ability. Text must be legible, contrast must be strong, touch targets must be forgiving, and the app must work well with screen readers and platform accessibility tools from day one, not as a retrofit.

**Reliability**
A shared reminder platform earns trust by never silently failing. If BuzzMe says it will remind a family about something, it must — missed notifications don't just cause inconvenience, they break the core promise of the product and the trust between the people relying on it.

**Trustworthiness**
Because BuzzMe sits inside people's family and personal relationships, it must never feel exploitative, manipulative, or attention-seeking. No dark patterns, no engagement-maximizing notification tricks, no guilt-based nudges.

## 13. Risks and Product Anti-Patterns We Must Avoid

- **Feature creep toward "everything app."** The most likely way BuzzMe dies is by slowly absorbing calendar, task-management, and chat features until it becomes a diluted version of three other apps and the best version of none.
- **Notification fatigue.** If BuzzMe becomes another source of noisy, poorly-timed alerts, it inherits the exact problem it was built to solve — reminders people learn to ignore.
- **Corporate tone creep.** As the team grows and more "professional" contributors join, there will be constant quiet pressure toward productivity-tool language and patterns. This must be actively resisted.
- **Over-configuration.** Every settings toggle added "to give users control" is a small tax on the zero-learning-curve promise. Configuration should be a last resort, not a first instinct.
- **AI dependency.** BuzzMe must be completely successful if AI never ships at all. The moment any core workflow requires an AI provider to work — not just to be faster — the product has taken on a fragility and a vendor dependency it doesn't need. If AI is ever added, it is one more way to fill in a form that already works perfectly without it.
- **Privacy erosion for growth's sake.** Any pressure to loosen default privacy (e.g., to boost invite virality or discovery metrics) must be treated as a direct threat to the product's core trust proposition.
- **Treating boards like workspaces.** Any pull toward roles, permissions matrices, admin panels, or org-chart thinking is a signal the product is drifting from "family and friends" toward "enterprise tool" — a different, unwanted category entirely.
- **Losing the emotional register.** If growth metrics ever start being optimized ahead of the feeling of warmth and care described in Section 9, the product has lost its reason for existing, even if usage numbers look healthy.
- **Cross-platform neglect.** Letting one platform (typically iOS) get disproportionate love at the expense of Android or web breaks the shared premise at its root — a shared memory tool that excludes some of the people you're trying to remember with isn't shared at all.

---

*This document is the reference point for "does this belong in BuzzMe." When a decision is unclear, the test is simple: does it make remembering together easier, calmer, and more human — or does it make BuzzMe feel like one more app to manage?*
